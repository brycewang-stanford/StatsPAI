## Notation

`n` rows; `D` treated-group dummy; `T` post-period dummy (repeated cross
sections); `X` covariates with a leading intercept; `w` user weights divided by
their sample mean (so `E_n[w] = 1`; the DRDID help says weights "are normalized
and therefore enforced to have mean 1"). `E_n` is the sample mean.

A **Hajek mean** is `theta(a, Z) = E_n[a Z] / E_n[a]` for non-negative arm
weights `a`; its influence function is `a (Z - theta) / E_n[a]`. A nuisance
parameter `beta` with influence function `l_i` (a k-vector per row) adds
`l_i' g` to the estimator's influence function, `g` being the gradient of the
estimator with respect to `beta` (delta method; Appendix A).

Nuisance influence functions:

- Weighted OLS of `Y` on `X` over rows `R`:
  `l_i = (E_n[w 1_R X X'])^{-1} w_i 1_R,i X_i e_i`.
- Weighted logit MLE of `D` on `X`:
  `l_i = (E_n[w p (1-p) X X'])^{-1} w_i (D_i - p_i) X_i`.
- Inverse probability tilting (IPT): `argmax E_n[w (D X'g - (1-D) exp(X'g))]`,
  `p = Lambda(X'g)` (paper Section 3.1). No influence term: its first-order
  conditions remove the estimation effect (Theorems 2-3).

Propensity odds `o = p / (1 - p)`. **Trimming** (DRDID `trim.level`, default
0.995): a comparison row with `p >= trim_level` gets weight zero in the
comparison-arm weights `a_0`. Treated rows are never trimmed. Trimming does not
touch the outcome regressions or the propensity fit (black box: matches R
`att.inf.func` to 1e-9 on a design with 6 / 13 trimmed controls, the residual
being R's logit stopping rule). Repeated-cross-section scores are capped at
`1 - 1e-6`; the CS panel engines keep using `_estimate_pscore`, which clips to
`[1e-6, 1 - 1e-6]`.

**Standard error**: `sqrt(mean(IF^2) / n)` for every estimator except the TWFE
regression, whose SE is `sd(IF) / sqrt(n)` with the `n - 1` divisor (black box:
both identities hold to 1e-15 in R's output).

## Panel estimators (on `dY = Y_1 - Y_0`)

Arms: `a_1 = w D`, `a_0 = keep w o (1 - D)`.

| Estimator | Point estimate | Influence function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OR (`reg_did_panel`, eq. 2.2) | `theta(a_1, dY - X'b)`, `b` = WLS of `dY` on controls | Hajek term `-` `l_b' E_n[a_1 X]/E_n[a_1]` |
| Hajek IPW (`std_ipw_did_panel`) | `theta(a_1, dY) - theta(a_0, dY)` | Hajek terms `-` `l_g' E_n[a_0 (dY - theta_0) X]/E_n[a_0]` |
| Abadie IPW (`ipw_did_panel`, eq. 2.4) | `E_n[(a_1 - a_0) dY] / E_n[a_1]` | `((a_1 - a_0) dY - tau a_1)/E_n[a_1]` `-` `l_g' E_n[a_0 dY X]/E_n[a_1]` |
| DR (`drdid_panel`, eq. 3.1) | `theta(a_1, r) - theta(a_0, r)`, `r = dY - X'b` | Hajek terms + `l_b'(E_n[a_0 X]/E_n[a_0] - E_n[a_1 X]/E_n[a_1])` `-` `l_g' E_n[a_0 (r - theta_0) X]/E_n[a_0]` |
| Improved DR (`drdid_imp_panel`, Sec. 3.1) | as DR with `g` from IPT and `b` from WLS with weights `w o` on controls | Hajek terms only |

`d a_0 / d g = a_0 X` because `d o / d g = o X` under the logit.

## Repeated cross-section estimators

Cell arms: `a_{1,t} = w D 1{T=t}`, `a_{0,t} = keep w o (1-D) 1{T=t}`,
`a_D = w D`. Outcome regressions `mu_{d,t}` are fit separately on each
(D, T) cell (OLS with weights `w`; for the improved estimators the two
comparison cells use weights `w o` with `o` from IPT). `mu_{0,Y} = T mu_{0,1} +
(1-T) mu_{0,0}`.

- **DR, not locally efficient** (`drdid_rc1`, eq. 3.3):
  `tau_1 = [theta(a_11, Y - mu_01) - theta(a_10, Y - mu_00)] -
  [theta(a_01, Y - mu_01) - theta(a_00, Y - mu_00)]`.
- **DR, locally efficient** (`drdid_rc`, eq. 3.4):
  `tau_2 = tau_1 + [theta(a_D, mu_11 - mu_01) - theta(a_11, mu_11 - mu_01)]
  - [theta(a_D, mu_10 - mu_00) - theta(a_10, mu_10 - mu_00)]`.
- **Improved** (`drdid_imp_rc1` / `drdid_imp_rc`, eqs. 3.9-3.10): same formulas
  with IPT scores and odds-weighted comparison regressions; influence function
  = Hajek terms only.
- **Hajek IPW** (`std_ipw_did_rc`): `[theta(a_11, Y) - theta(a_10, Y)] -
  [theta(a_01, Y) - theta(a_00, Y)]`; influence adds the logit term through
  `a_01`, `a_00`.
- **Abadie IPW** (`ipw_did_rc`, eq. 2.3): with `A = E_n[(a_1 - a_0) T Y]`,
  `B = E_n[(a_1 - a_0)(1-T) Y]`, `P = E_n[a_1]`, `L_1 = E_n[w T]`,
  `L_0 = E_n[w (1-T)]`: `tau = (A / L_1 - B / L_0) / P`, here
  `a_1 = w D`, `a_0 = keep w o (1-D)`. Influence by the delta method in the
  five moments, the post and pre shares `L_1`, `L_0` being **separate** sample
  moments with influence `w T - L_1` and `w (1-T) - L_0` (the normalized
  weights are treated as fixed). Black box: with weights this is the only
  convention that reproduces R (`w T - L_1` alone misses by 2-6% of the SE);
  without weights all conventions coincide.
- **OR** (`reg_did_rc`, eq. 2.2): `theta(a_11, Y) - theta(a_10, Y) -
  theta(a_D, mu_01 - mu_00)`; influence adds the two comparison-cell OLS terms.
- **TWFE** (`twfe_did_rc`, eq. 2.5): weighted OLS of `Y` on
  `(1, D, T, D T, X)`; the coefficient on `D T`, influence = the OLS influence
  row for that coefficient.

**Cell regressions that cannot be fit.** A cell with fewer rows than
regressors raises `DataInsufficient` (existing contract,
`test_drdid_raises_when_the_outcome_regressions_are_not_identified`). A cell
whose design is singular (a covariate collinear with earlier columns, e.g.
constant within the cell): the improved estimators drop that column from the
cell's regression (columns screened in order); the traditional DR and OR
estimators raise `MethodIncompatibility`. Black box (design 4): DRDID's
improved estimators give exactly the drop-column numbers (1e-15), its other
regression-based estimators stop with "design matrix ... is singular".

### The pre-period comparison regression in `drdid_rc` / `drdid_rc1`

The delta method (and Appendix B of the paper) gives the `mu_00` estimation
effect of `tau_1` as `+ l_00' (E_n[a_10 X]/E_n[a_10] - E_n[a_00 X]/E_n[a_00])`.
R `DRDID` 1.3.0's `att.inf.func` carries this term with the **opposite sign**
(black box: on our designs `R_IF - delta_IF` regresses on `l_00` with
R^2 = 1 - 3e-15 and coefficient exactly `-2 (M_10 - M_00)`; the `mu_01`,
treated-cell and propensity terms agree, and the extra locally efficient terms
keep the delta-method sign). The two conventions coincide asymptotically when
the propensity model is correct (`M_10 - M_00 -> 0`).

Monte Carlo (1,000 draws, n = 2,000, misspecified propensity score, correct
comparison outcome regressions, true ATT 1;
`tests/reference_parity/_fixtures/_drdid_rc_pre_regression_mc.py`):

| | sd of estimates | mean SE, delta (coverage) | mean SE, reference (coverage) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `drdid_rc1` | 0.2770 | 0.2790 (0.952) | 0.1876 (0.813) |
| `drdid_rc` | 0.2769 | 0.2786 (0.952) | 0.1879 (0.815) |

The rewrite keeps the reference sign as the default so that no published
number moves in a license rewrite (`variance_convention="reference"` in
`_rcs.drdid_rc` / `drdid_rc1` paths). `variance_convention="delta"` gives the
paper's influence function. **Owner decision pending**: switch the default
(a correctness fix that moves the SE of `sp.drdid(method="trad")` on repeated
cross sections and of `callaway_santanna(panel=False, estimator="dr")`).

## Callaway-Sant'Anna aggregation weights

An aggregate `theta = sum_k w_k ATT_k` over cells `k` with
`w_k = p_k / sum_j p_j`, `p_k = E_n[omega 1{G = g_k}]` the (weighted) share of
cell `k`'s cohort, has estimated weights. Delta method with
`ind_ik = omega_i 1{G_i = g_k}`, `S = sum_j p_j`:

`IF(w_k)_i = (ind_ik - p_k) / S - p_k sum_j (ind_ij - p_j) / S^2`

(`weight_influence(pg, ind)` returns this `n x K` matrix, zero when `S = 0`).
Rows sum to zero across `k` (the weights sum to one) and columns average to
zero. The aggregated influence function of row `r` of the weight matrix `W` is
`IF @ W_r + IF(w)[:, keep] @ ATT[keep]`, `keep` = cells with non-zero weight
(`_aggregated_influence`). Cells of a single cohort get `IF(w) = 0`
identically, which is why per-cohort averages carry no extra term.
